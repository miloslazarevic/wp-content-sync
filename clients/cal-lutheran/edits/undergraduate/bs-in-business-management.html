What You’ll Learn in the Business Management Degree

The BS in Business Management builds skills that carry across industries: how the core parts of a company work, how to turn plans into results and how to make sound decisions under changing market conditions.

  • A working command of the core business functions, from finance to marketing, and how they fit together
  • The analytical skills to break down a problem and decide what to do about it
  • The quantitative skills to make sense of growing amounts of business data
  • Writing and speaking skills that hold up in a room full of decision-makers
  • A grounding in how to execute strategy, coordinate resources, and manage timelines
  • The performance managements skills to keep a team running smoothly

Who Is This Program For?

Business management students come from all kinds of backgrounds. Some have been supervising teams or managing day-to-day operations for years and want a degree to make more authoritative decisions in the work they already do. Others are reaching for a first management role.

We also support students who are focused on their own business — whether they’re running one already or getting ready to start — and want a real grounding in how business works.

Most students are earning their degree while working, supporting families and keeping the rest of life going, and they often are returning to school after years away.

If that sounds like you, the only requirements to apply are a high school diploma or GED certificate (2.25 grade point average or higher) and at least 24 credits of college credit from an accredited institution. Visit our admissions page for more on what you need.

Total Credits to Complete the Degree

To graduate, students must complete 124 credits minimum. This total includes:

  • Major courses: At least 57 credits are completed through business management major courses. Out of these, at least 30 credits must be upper-division courses (these courses are identified by course numbers in the 300 and 400 ranges).
  • General education and other courses: The remaining credits can consist of general education courses, additional major courses if desired and any transferred credits.

Frequently Asked Questions

A degree completion program is a bachelor’s degree built for people who already have some college credit. Instead of starting over, you transfer in the courses you’ve already earned and build your degree plan from there.

For the Bachelor of Science in Business Management, every incoming student brings at least 24 credits from a previous accredited associate or undergraduate program, and many bring more credits.

The difference from a traditional bachelor’s degree is mostly who it’s for and how it’s run — not the quality of the education. A general program is built around full-time students who are usually new to college. Cal Lutheran’s program is built around working adults who have some college behind them and a full life around them.

We know our students have to balance their degree around jobs, families and everything else, so you can complete the program fully online at your own pace, join an optional weekly live session in the evening or attend evening classes in person at our Thousand Oaks campus.

Student support services are built for adult learners coming back to school too: academic advisors know your situation and personalized career counseling when you need it.

For someone who started a degree years ago and always meant to finish, a completion program is the most direct way back.

The two areas overlap, but they emphasize different things.

  • Business administration takes a wider view, with a focus on how a whole organization is structured and how its functions fit together for long-term success.
  • Business management is more focused on the day-to-day work of running operations and making sure teams have what they need to support that success.

Cal Lutheran’s program is business management, which means the emphasis is practical and execution-centered. You’ll build a foundation in the core business functions, from finance to marketing, and develop the skills to turn plans into results, make sound decisions and communicate them clearly.

If your goal is to supervise teams, manage responsibilities and understand how different parts of a business work together, business management is likely the closer fit.

You can read a more detailed comparison in our article on business management vs. business administration.

Business management is a fit for people, project, and operations managers who ensure that day-to-day work is completed correctly, on time and within budget. It is a good fit if you want a wide foundation in business or are preparing for management roles that require you to understand several parts of an organization, including finance, marketing, analytics and strategy.

Organizational leadership focuses more deeply on people. You’ll study how to motivate teams, build trust, communicate across differences, navigate conflict, lead through change and make ethical decisions. It is a good fit if you see yourself leading organizational improvement and developing company cultures, or if you want to better understand how motivation and coaching supports stronger organizations.

If you want a broader business foundation, we recommend the BS in Business Management. If you want to focus on motivating people and strengthening organizations from the inside, the BS in Organizational Leadership may be the better fit.

You can read a more detailed comparison in our article on organizational leadership versus business management.
