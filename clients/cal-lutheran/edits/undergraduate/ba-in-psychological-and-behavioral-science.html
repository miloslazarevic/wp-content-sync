What You’ll Learn in the Psychological and Behavioral Science Degree

The BA in Psychological and Behavioral Science program takes a scientific approach to human thought and behavior, alongside the critical thinking and communication skills that psychology careers and graduate programs both expect. By graduation you’ll have:

  • A command of the major theories and findings in psychology
  • The research knowledge to ask good questions, analyze results and judge the conclusions others draw
  • The ability to apply psychological concepts to real individual and community challenges
  • The writing and speaking skills to present your thinking clearly and professionally

Who Is This Program For?

This program fits people who want to understand human behavior and put that understanding to work.

Some already serve in high-impact roles in education, social services, health care or human resources. This degree offers them the credential and the academic theory to improve on what they do every day.

Others are preparing for graduate school in psychology, social work or a related field and need a strong undergraduate foundation first.

Total Credits to Complete the Degree

To graduate, students must complete 124 credits minimum. This total includes:

  • Major courses: At least 48 credits are completed through psychology and behavioral science major courses. Out of these, at least 39 credits must be upper-division courses (these courses are identified by course numbers in the 300 and 400 ranges).
  • General education and other courses: The remaining credits can consist of general education courses, additional major courses if desired and any transferred credits.

Career Outcomes With a BA in Psychological and Behavioral Science

A psychology background has wide applications in education, health care, business, social services, law and research. With a bachelor’s degree, graduates are prepared for roles such as:

  • Social services coordinator
  • Educational advisor
  • Research assistant
  • Behavioral health technician
  • Human resources specialist
  • Research analyst

This degree can also serve as foundation for further study, as graduates can use this degree to apply for a graduate study in clinical psychology, counseling, social work or behavior analysis. From there they may go on to licensed and senior roles in the field.

The critical thinking and research skills you build here are exactly what employers and graduate programs look for, which is why the degree works as both a standalone credential and a strong first step toward a master’s or doctorate.

Frequently Asked Questions

A degree completion program is a bachelor’s degree built for people who already have some college credit. Instead of starting over, you transfer in the courses you’ve already earned and build your degree plan from there.

For the Bachelor of Arts in Psychological and Behavioral Science, every incoming student brings at least 24 credit hours from a previous accredited associate or undergraduate program, and many bring more credits.

The difference from a traditional bachelor’s degree is mostly who it’s for and how it’s run — not the quality of the education. A general program is built around full-time students who are usually new to college. Cal Lutheran’s degree program is built around working adults who have some college behind them and a full life around them.

We know our students have to balance their degree around jobs, families and everything else, so you can complete the program fully online at your own pace, join an optional weekly live session in the evening or attend evening classes in person at our Thousand Oaks campus.

Student support services are built for adult learners coming back to school too: academic advisors know your situation and personalized career counseling when you need it.

For someone who started a degree years ago and always meant to finish, a completion program is the most direct way back.

The degree studies both sides of human experience: the mental processes behind how we think and feel, and the behavior that follows.

With the degree’s scientific approach, you have a strong understanding of the biological, environmental and social factors that shape both thoughts and behaviors, across subfields like cognition, development, social psychology and psychopathology.

That combination is useful in any people-centered field, from counseling and education to human resources and research. It also prepares you well for graduate study, where both theory and the research skills carry over.
