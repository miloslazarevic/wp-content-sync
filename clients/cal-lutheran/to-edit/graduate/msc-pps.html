K-12 school counselors shape generations. They advocate for students who can’t yet advocate for themselves and help students understand who they could become. The Master of Science in Counseling and Pupil Personnel Services (PPS) gives you the counseling skills, field experience and the credential to do that work in California schools.

The coursework builds your expertise in counseling theory, child and adolescent development and the legal and ethical frameworks of K-12 education.

You’ll also spend significant time in K-12 schools completing a supervised practicum and two field studies under credentialed school counselors. When you graduate, you’re eligible for California’s PPS Credential in School Counseling.

The MS in Counseling (PPS) program runs as a cohort at our Thousand Oaks campus. Classes meet in the evening on weeknights, so you can complete the degree while continuing to work.

Know Yourself

The PPS curriculum is built around the cultural proficiency model, which asks you to examine your own background and identity first. Once you understand how your own experiences shape what you see and how you respond, you can recognize what’s happening for a student whose experiences are different from yours and respond in a way that actually helps.

Know Your Students

K-12 students don’t always have the language for what they’re going through. The program grounds you in child and adolescent development, the ASCA framework for school counseling and the individual and group counseling skills you’ll use every day. You’ll learn to listen for what students aren’t saying as carefully as what they are saying.

Know the System

School counselors don’t work alone. You’ll coordinate with teachers, families, administrators to connect students to the resources they need across the K-12 system and beyond. The program prepares you to do that systems-level work in alignment with the legal and ethical frameworks that govern K-12 education in California.

What Will You Learn in the Master’s in School Counseling Program?

The MS in Counseling (PPS) is a 48-credit program that can be completed in three years, with students in the standard curriculum taking two courses per term.

The program runs as a cohort, so you’ll move through the curriculum alongside the same group of classmates. Two specializations are available: the Child Welfare and Attendance Specialization adds three credits, and the College Counseling Specialization adds twelve credits.

Sample courses in the PPS degree program include:

  • Lifespan Human Development
  • Law and Ethics in School Counseling
  • Counseling Theory and Practice
  • Microskills in Counseling
  • Group Process and Leadership
  • Career and College Development and Planning
  • Diverse Perspectives in Education and Culturally Proficient Leadership
  • Field Studies in School Counseling I and II (supervised practicum and field experience across two school levels)

Who Is the Counseling and Pupil Personnel Services (PPS) Degree For?

This program is for people who want to work as K-12 school counselors, particularly in California. It leads to eligibility for California’s pupil personnel services credential​ in school counseling, which is required for these positions.

The students who come to the MS in Counseling (PPS) arrive with a range of starting points behind them, from teaching and classroom aide work to careers outside education entirely, but they share a reason for being here: they want to be the counselor a student remembers.

They also share a commitment to serving their community. Many want to work in the schools and districts where they grew up. Others want to serve students whose circumstances have been overlooked by the systems around them.

Since this program is offered in-person only, at the Thousand Oaks campus, students will need to be able to commute for one evening a week of on-campus classes.

If your goal is to work with college students, the Master of Science in Counseling and College Student Personnel is designed around that age group.

What Can You Do With a Master’s in School Counseling?

School counselors work with students at every stage of K-12. Elementary counselors work with kids at the beginning of their sense of self. Middle school counselors work with students as their world expands and things get more complicated. High school counselors work with students navigating college applications, first jobs and hard decisions about who they want to become.

The master’s in school counseling degree leads to meaningful work, and the need is real. California’s student-to-counselor ratio is among the highest in the country, and the state has been investing in expanding the school counseling workforce. Public schools across California need counselors who can support students academically, socially and emotionally.

The degree also qualifies you for California’s Pupil Personnel Services Credential in School Counseling, which is required for K-12 counseling positions in California public schools. So you’ll have the skills and the needed qualifications to get a job in the region.

For K-12 public schools, no. California’s Pupil Personnel Services Credential in School Counseling is required by the state for anyone working as a school counselor in a public school district, from kindergarten through 12th grade. The credential is issued by the California Commission on Teacher Credentialing and is a non-negotiable qualification for public school counseling positions.

Private schools aren’t subject to this requirement under California Education Code, and hiring practices vary from school to school. That said, holding the PPS Credential widens the kinds of positions available to you across both public and private K-12 schools in the state.

For school counseling roles outside K-12 schools, other credentials or degrees may apply. Community college counselors, for example, are governed by different state regulations and typically need a master’s in counseling or a closely related field rather than the PPS Credential specifically.

To earn California’s Pupil Personnel Services (PPS) Credential in School Counseling, you need to complete four things:

  • A bachelor’s degree from a regionally accredited college or university
  • A CTC-approved graduate program in school counseling, typically 48 units or more, including a supervised practicum
  • The Basic Skills Requirement, often satisfied through the CBEST exam or their undergraduate studies
  • A background check and Live Scan fingerprinting

Once these are complete, your graduate program recommends you to the California Commission on Teacher Credentialing (CTC), which reviews the recommendation and issues the credential.

Cal Lutheran’s MS in Counseling (PPS) is CTC-approved and structured to meet the education and supervised experience requirements. Students who complete the program are eligible to be recommended for the PPS Credential in School Counseling.
