What is the benefit of a combined accounting and financial analysis degree?

The benefit is range. You graduate able to work across the whole life of financial information, which opens more roles and more room to grow than either skill alone.

Accounting is the practice of recording and reporting financial information accurately. Financial analysis is the work of interpreting that information to guide decisions, like whether a company can afford to expand or where it's losing money.

Many programs treat these as separate tracks. Here they're taught together, so you get a fuller picture of how organizations handle money.

On the accounting side, courses like Intermediate Financial Accountingand Principles of Auditing and Assurancegive you the technical command to prepare those records accurately.

On the analysis side, courses such as Data Analytics in AccountingandFinancial Statement Analysisteach you to interpret financial information.

That combination is what allows an accountant to advise on the numbers in addition to recording them.

A note on requirements:Passing the exam is just one step toward becoming a licensed CPA. The full requirements are set by each state, so they vary depending on where you plan to be licensed.

In California:

  • You currently need a bachelor's degree, 24 semester units in accounting subjects and 24 in business-related subjects to sit for the exam, with some additional coursework, including ethics units, required for the license itself.
  • California is also moving to an updated set of rules that simplifies this, bringing the education required for the exam and the license into closer alignment and adding flexibility on the work-experience side. You can review both the current and updated rules in this CPA Exam quick tips guide.
  • Because the state is in the middle of that transition, it's worth confirming the current requirements before you plan around them. Make sure to check the California Board of Accountancy site.

If you plan to be licensed in another state, the National Association of State Boards of Accountancy maintains the requirements for every state at nasba.org.

What's the difference between business management and business administration?

The two areas overlap, but they emphasize different things.

  • Business administration takes a wider view, with a focus on how a whole organization is structured and how its functions fit together for long-term success.
  • Business management is more focused on the day-to-day work of running operations and making sure teams have what they need to support that success.

Cal Lutheran's program is business management, which means the emphasis is practical and execution-centered. You'll build a foundation in the core business functions, from finance to marketing, and develop the skills to turn plans into results, make sound decisions and communicate them clearly.

If your goal is to supervise teams, manage responsibilities and understand how different parts of a business work together, business management is likely the closer fit.

You can read a more detailed comparison in our article on business management vs. business administration .

What's the difference between business management and organizational leadership degrees?Business management is a fit for people, project, and operations managers who ensure that day-to-day work is completed correctly, on time and within budget. It is a good fit if you want a wide foundation in business or are preparing for management roles that require you to understand several parts of an organization, including finance, marketing, analytics and strategy.

Organizational leadership focuses more deeply on people. You'll study how to motivate teams, build trust, communicate across differences, navigate conflict, lead through change and make ethical decisions. It is a good fit if you see yourself leading organizational improvement and developing company cultures, or if you want to better understand how motivation and coaching supports stronger organizations.

If you want a broader business foundation, we recommend the BS in Business Management. If you want to focus on motivating people and strengthening organizations from the inside, the BS in Organizational Leadershipmay be the better fit.

You can read a more detailed comparison in our article on organizational leadership versus business management .

What's the difference between criminology and criminal justice?

They're related, but they ask different questions.

  • Criminal justice tends to focus on the system itself: how policing, advocacy, the courts and corrections operates together.
  • Criminology looks at the bigger picture: why crime happens, how it affects victims and offenders, and how factors like race, gender and inequality shape people's experience of the system.

This program is built on the criminology side, but includes the foundations of criminal justice. You study how the system works, but also why it works the way it does and what could make it more fair. That's useful whether you want to work within the system or alongside it in advocacy and support.

Getting Started

What is a degree completion program? How does this compare to a general bachelor's degree experience?

A degree completion program is a bachelor's degree built for people who already have some college credit. Instead of starting over, you transfer in the courses you've already earned and build your degree plan from there.

For the Bachelor's Degree for Professionals, every incoming student brings at least 24 credit hours from a previous accredited associate or undergraduate program, and many bring more credits.

The difference from a traditional bachelor's degree is mostly who it's for and how it's run — not the quality of the education. A general program is built around full-time students who are usually new to college. Cal Lutheran's degree program is built around working adults who have some college behind them and a full life around them.

We know our students have to balance their degree around jobs, families and everything else, so most programs run fully online with an optional weekly live session in the evening. Two programs also offer in-person evening classes at our Thousand Oaks campus.

Student support services are built for adult learners coming back to school too: academic advisors know your situation and personalized career counseling when you need it.

For someone who started a degree years ago and always meant to finish, a completion program is the most direct way back.

You can find out more about applying to these degrees, including info on credit transfers, on our admissions page.

Who is the Bachelor's Degree for Professionals designed for?

These majors are designed for adults who have earned college credit before and are ready to finish their bachelor's degree.

Instead of centering the experience of first-time college students coming straight from high school, these programs are built for people who are returning to school with work experience, responsibilities and a clearer sense of what they want their degree to do for their careers.

Beyond that, there is no single type of student. Some are early in their careers, others have decades of experience behind them. Many are balancing school with a full-time job, family responsibilities or both. Some know exactly where they want their degree to take them, while others are still deciding what comes next.

That range adds value to the classroom. Students bring real work and life experience into discussions, and faculty design their courses around adult learners, not straight-from-high-school students.

My previous college credit is in a different subject/major. Does that limit which major I can choose now?

No. You can choose any of the six bachelor's degrees, regardless of what you studied before you came to Cal Lutheran.

An enrollment specialist can help you see how your existing credits apply to the major you choose — call us at 805-521-9856.
