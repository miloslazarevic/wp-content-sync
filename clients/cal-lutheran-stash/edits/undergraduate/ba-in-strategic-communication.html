Communication is changing fast. AI tools, algorithmic platforms, and information overload are reshaping how organizations and individuals communicate every day. Cal Lutheran's Bachelor of Arts in Strategic Communication prepares working adults to lead in this environment.

You'll build a strong foundation in communication theory, information literacy, research, and persuasion, and you'll learn to think critically about how AI and emerging technologies are transforming professional communication. Whether you're looking to advance in your current organization or open doors to new career paths, this degree gives you the analytical and strategic skills to communicate with purpose.

Part of California Lutheran University's Bachelor's Degree for Professionals, this degree completion program is built for learners with previous college credit. You'll learn in small classes with faculty who get to know you, value the experience you bring and support you as you finish your degree.

What You'll Learn in the Strategic Communication Degree

The program is hands-on, starting with how communication works in theory and moving to how that translates into real campaigns. By graduation you'll be able to:

  • Understand communication as a discipline, including its central questions and key theories
  • Craft messages that fit a specific audience, purpose and setting, with real practice doing it
  • Analyze how messages land and the wider influence communication has on people
  • Apply ethical principles to your communication work
  • Recognize and account for individual and cultural differences in how you communicate

Who Is This Program For?

The BA in Strategic Communication program fits people who already communicate as part of their work and want to get formal skills to enhance their work.

Some of our students manage social media, write for their organization or handle public messaging, and want the theory and the credential to back up what they already do. Others are moving toward communication, marketing or public relations roles for the first time.

Clear communication matters in every job, so the degree can also suit people in other fields who want it as a strength on their resume.

What all our students share is that they know communicating well is a skill worth having. To apply, you need a high school diploma or GED certificate and at least 24 credits of college credit from an accredited institution. Visit our admissions page for more on what you need.

Career Outcomes With a BA in Strategic Communication

Strategic communication graduates go on to communications roles inside companies across just about every industry. They might be a social media manager, content strategist, communications coordinator or PR lead at an automaker, a hospital, a bank or a tech firm. Nearly every organization needs people who can shape a message and connect with an audience, which is why these skills travel so widely.

Other graduates go on to work in agencies, media or other creative settings where communication is the whole business.

And the skills reach past jobs with “communications” in the title. Strong communicators tend to get further in almost any career, because knowing how to read a crowded media environment, understand an audience and make your point clearly makes you better at the work itself.

Frequently Asked Questions

A degree completion program is a bachelor's degree built for people who already have some college credit. Instead of starting over, you transfer in the courses you've already earned and build your degree plan from there.

For the Bachelor of Arts in Strategic Communication, every incoming student brings at least 24 credit hours from a previous accredited associate or undergraduate program, and many bring more credits.

We know our students have to balance their degree around jobs, families and everything else, so the program runs fully online with the option to join a weekly live session in the evening.

Student support services are built for adult learners coming back to school too: academic advisors know your situation and personalized career counseling when you need it.

For someone who started a degree years ago and always meant to finish, a completion program is the most direct way back.

Public relations is one part of strategic communication. PR focuses on an organization's reputation and its relationships with the public and the media. Strategic communication is a wider field. It covers PR along with marketing messaging, internal communication, digital content and the overall strategy behind how an organization communicates.

Studying strategic communication means you learn PR as one tool among several, plus the theory and analysis to decide which approach fits a given goal. That broader foundation works in PR roles and beyond them.
