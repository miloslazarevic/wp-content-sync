The justice system affects people at the most difficult moments of their lives. Working fairly and effectively in this field means looking beyond laws and procedures to the communities, histories and human experiences behind them.

California Lutheran University's Bachelor of Science in Criminology and Justice Studies looks at justice from every side of the system. You'll study how policing, courts and corrections actually function, and how individual and cultural differences shape the way people experience them. You'll also study the causes of crime, victim advocacy and how people successfully reenter society after incarceration.

Part of Cal Lutheran's Bachelor's Degree for Professionals, this degree completion program is built for learners who have some previous college credit. You'll learn in small classes with faculty who get to know you, value the experience you bring and support you as you finish what you started.

At Cal Lutheran, we pride ourselves on providing personal attention to each of our students. Our classes are intentionally kept small so that you can connect with your instructors and peers. You'll also have access to a personal academic counselor who will guide and support you every step of the way.

What You'll Learn in the Criminology and Justice Studies Degree

The program pairs criminological theory with the realities of how justice works in practice. By graduation you'll have:

  • A clear grasp of the major theories and concepts in criminology and justice studies
  • The research methods to analyze justice issues and reach conclusions you can defend
  • The ability to apply what you learn to real situations, from local communities to global systems
  • An understanding of how individual and cultural differences shape people's experience of the justice system
  • The critical thinking to engage in hard questions about fairness, accountability and what justice should look like

Who Is This Program For?

Our students are mostly working adults balancing school with family and a full-time job. Some already work in law enforcement, the courts or corrections.

Others work in social services roles, bringing experience that overlaps with the field in different ways. And some come to the field through personal experience, community advocacy or a search for work that feels like it matters, even if their current job has nothing to do with justice or criminology.

To apply, you need a high school diploma or GED certificate and at least 24 credits of college credit from an accredited institution. Visit our admissions page for more on what you need.

Total Credits to Complete the Degree

To graduate, students must complete 124 credits minimum. This total includes:

  • Major courses: At least 45 credits are completed through criminology and justice studies major courses. Out of these, at least 36 credits must be upper-division courses (these courses are identified by course numbers in the 300 and 400 ranges).
  • General education and other courses: The remaining credits can consist of general education courses, additional major courses if desired and any transferred credits.

Career Outcomes With a BS in Criminology and Justice Studies

Research demonstrates the benefits of re-entry, rehabilitation and supporting the people who move through the system, but the justice system can be slow to adapt these as policy. That's why we need well-educated, degree-holding professionals who can make progress across enforcement and the courts as well as advocacy and social services. Common roles include:

  • Probation officer
  • Parole officer
  • Victim advocate
  • Community outreach and advocacy roles
  • Re-entry specialist
  • Corrections officer
  • Police officer
  • Social services caseworker
The degree is also a strong foundation for law school or graduate study. Whichever direction graduates take, they leave understanding justice only works when it holds people accountable while still treating them as people.

Frequently Asked Questions

A degree completion program is a bachelor's degree built for people who already have some college credit. Instead of starting over, you transfer in the courses you've already earned and build your degree plan from there.

For the Bachelor of Science in Criminology and Justice Studies, every incoming student brings at least 24 credits from a previous accredited associate or undergraduate program, and many bring more credits.

The difference from a traditional bachelor's degree is mostly who it's for and how it's run — not the quality of the education. A general program is built around full-time students who are usually new to college. Cal Lutheran's degree program is built around working adults who have some college behind them and a full life around them.

We know our students have to balance their degree around jobs, families and everything else, so the program runs fully online with the option to join a weekly live session in the evening.

Student support services are built for adult learners coming back to school too: academic advisors know your situation and personalized career counseling when you need it.

For someone who started a degree years ago and always meant to finish, a completion program is the most direct way back.
